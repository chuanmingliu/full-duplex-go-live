golive — simulated full duplex

Sent as session.instructions at session start. Leave empty to use the server's. Keep it short — the conversational layer has a small context window, and detailed procedure belongs in the backend prompt, not here.

Spoken the moment the session opens, before you say anything. Empty means the assistant waits to be spoken to. You can interrupt a greeting like any other turn.

One per line, or comma-separated. Say one of these while the assistant is talking and it keeps talking — the utterance is recognised, reported as golive.input_backchannel, and dropped rather than answered.
A real full-duplex model needs no list: it hears a murmur and knows to hold the floor. This is the blunt stand-in. Anything not on the list still interrupts, and does so on the first syllable that rules a backchannel out — "嗯，等一下" cuts in at 等.
Empty the box to switch it off entirely, back to any-speech-interrupts.

cut — stop immediately, mid-word. Right when the user expects to redirect a fast assistant.
finish sentence — complete the sentence being spoken, then answer the new query. Usually right on a phone line, where a mid-word stop reads as a dropped call.
queue — say everything first. This is what a half-duplex cascade does; it talks over the caller and answers a question they have already moved past. Kept for comparison.

Talk over the assistant while it is speaking — listen and speak light up together, and the transcript records only the part you actually heard.

Hear nothing? Press Test sound. It pushes a tone through the exact playback path the assistant's audio uses, so a silent tone means the tab or the output device is the problem, not the service. On macOS, opening the microphone can flip a Bluetooth headset into its low-quality call mode and silence playback — switch Output to the built-in speakers if so.
